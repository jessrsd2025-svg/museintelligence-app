## 2. GENIUS Act fact sheet

**pp. 2–3.** Sourced in the document to Congress.gov, the White House, Morgan Lewis, WilmerHale, and Paul Hastings.

### Basic legislative facts

| Field | As stated in the document |
|---|---|
| Official title | Guiding and Establishing National Innovation for U.S. Stablecoins Act (GENIUS Act) |
| Bill number | S.1582, 119th Congress |
| Sponsor | Sen. Bill Hagerty (R-TN) |
| Policy area | Finance and Financial Sector |
| Status | Became Public Law No. 119-27 upon enactment on July 18, 2025 |

### What the Act does

Establishes the first comprehensive federal regulatory framework for "payment stablecoins" — digital assets designed for payments or settlement and redeemable at a fixed value (e.g., $1).

### Key provisions

- **Definition of payment stablecoins:** digital assets issued for payments or settlement and redeemable at a predetermined fixed amount.
- **Reserve requirements:** issuers must hold at least $1 of permitted (low-risk) reserves for every $1 of stablecoins issued.
- **Prohibitions on misrepresentation:** unlawful to claim stablecoins are backed by the full faith and credit of the United States or are FDIC/NCUA insured unless issued under this Act; violations carry civil and criminal penalties.
- **Governance and compliance:** restrictions on individuals convicted of certain financial crimes serving as officers or directors of stablecoin issuers.
- **Federal and state roles:** a pathway for dual federal and certified state supervision of payment stablecoin issuers.

### Legislative process

- Passed the Senate: June 17, 2025.
- Passed the House: July 17, 2025.
- Signed into law: July 18, 2025.

### Regulatory and industry impact

- Closes a longstanding federal regulatory gap in stablecoin issuance and custody, which previously relied on a patchwork of state licenses and guidance.
- Aims to boost transparency, consumer protection, and the U.S. dollar's position in digital finance.
- Establishes a Stablecoin Certification Review Committee to harmonize federal and qualifying state stablecoin regimes.

### Context and analysis

- Legitimizing stablecoins for everyday payments and global finance infrastructure.
- The reserve and regulation requirements seek to prevent instability risks observed in earlier crypto collapses.
- Debate remains over how federal and state roles will coexist in implementation.
- The "US Crypto Policy Tracker: Legislative Developments" is described as the entry point into a curated, continuously updated overview of U.S. crypto legislation, organized by topic (stablecoins, NFTs, DeFi, CBDCs) and jurisdiction (federal vs. state) — a way to visualize the evolving legal landscape for digital assets.

## 9. DTO overdose intelligence system (Baltimore / San Francisco)

**pp. 22–24.** Filed under "Jesse Marshall Renderings of Strategic Dynamics" and "Homeland Security, DHS, Cybersecurity, (and global security topics)".

### System purpose

"The Baltimore and San Francisco Sanctuary-Carveout DTO Fentanyl Overdose Intelligence System is designed to create a governance pipeline focused on distinguishing drug trafficking organization (DTO)-driven lethal overdose events from general overdoses. The system activates federal partners only when necessary, ensuring strict civil liberties protections and thorough auditability."

### Baltimore node

- **Node:** Baltimore_BPD_BCFD (LocalPublicSafetyNode)
- **Jurisdiction:** Baltimore City, MD
- **Sanctuary status:** Sanctuary with DTO Carveout
- **Governance model:** ODMAP_to_JTF_pipeline
- **Participating agencies:** Baltimore Police Department (BPD); Baltimore City Fire Department (BCFD/EMS)
- **Functions:** ODMAP data ingestion; clustering of open-air markets; DTO confidence scoring; CDC WONDER validation; COVID-era dashboard logic; immutable audit trails; PolicyZone constraints; alert thresholds for overdose spikes and DTO confidence.

### San Francisco evolution

- **Node:** SanFrancisco_SFPD_SFFD — progressed "from a simple policy constraint into a fully governed intelligence node, building on Baltimore's ODMAP/JTF framework."
- **Key features:** real-time detection of overdose clusters; visualization of DTO activity along supply chains; integration of EMS telemetry and on-scene alerts; management of sanctuary policy zones as variables, not binary settings.
- **Operational approach:** enforcement is triggered only when epidemiological trends, DTO indicators, and policy limits align, relying on established COVID-era dashboard logic. The system "intentionally avoids ICE sweeps and does not collect immigration status information. Every alert is documented, reviewed, and must comply with stringent governance."
- **Network integration:** San Francisco operates within a DTO–overdose response network connecting New York City, Chicago, and other cities, using cluster polygons, temporal sliders, and sanctuary overlays — "compatible with GraphRAG, Neo4j, and live dashboard solutions."

### Strategic collaboration

The initiative "seeks to balance civil liberties with safety by emphasizing semantic governance, spatial intelligence, and joint accountability across agencies," and welcomes experts in corridor strategy, DTO disruption, or enforcement overlays that respect sanctuary policies.
